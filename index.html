About me

“The best lessons aren’t at the finish line. They’re in the trying.”

I’m Arsya, a Communication and Community Development student at IPB University. I notice how a team is doing, not just whether the task is done: who’s stuck, who’s gone quiet, who needs a nudge or a thank-you.

I didn’t learn that from winning. My first year of university was a long run of applications that never made it past round one, across scholarships, organizations, and competitions. For a while I pulled back and watched friends move ahead from the sidelines.

What I did next is the part I’m proudest of. I stopped sitting with the disappointment and started asking what could be improved, then reached out to people far better than me for help. That’s how I found out I have agility: I can take a rejection, learn from it, and try again.

It shapes how I work with people. I know what it feels like to be the quiet one, so I check on the quiet ones. I know progress is messy, so I build small systems that make room for it.

Bounces back by reflecting

After a rejection I ask what can be improved, then try again. I don’t romanticize failing, I just refuse to stop at it.

Asks for help early

Reaching out to people far better than me was one of my best decisions. Asking is a skill, not a weakness.

People first

I track well-being and team dynamics next to deadlines, because tired teams quietly miss both.

Systems over heroics

Weekly reports, evaluation frameworks, reminder systems: small structures that keep work moving without drama.
